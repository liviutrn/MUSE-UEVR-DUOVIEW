**Evidence rule.** Every finding is labeled **[SOURCE-READ]** (pinned repository source, Epic documentation or an explicitly identified *inference from* those sources) or **[HEADSET-VERIFIED]** (Liviu's own reported measurement/observation). **NOT VERIFIED** always means no actual STALKER 2 GPU capture or in-headset confirmation for the proposed mechanism. ## Executive decision

**[HEADSET-VERIFIED]** At a **200×200 configured view** in GPU-bound STALKER 2, Liviu measured approximately **7 ms GPU for each separate render family** with little change as render resolution was varied, versus approximately **2.5 ms incremental GPU** for an extra view **within the same family**. These are user measurements, not profiler-derived per-pass GPU values, and the actual `FSceneTexturesConfig::Extent`, physical target extent, DLSS input/output sizes, shadow-page count, and view-state settings in that test are **NOT VERIFIED**.

**[SOURCE-READ — inference]** The observation is evidence for a large **per-renderer-submission / per-family work floor**, but **does not prove a truly constant 7 ms architectural fee**. A shadow-map pass at unchanged page resolution, a Nanite scene traversal with the same geometry, a Lumen Surface Cache update, a fixed-sized volumetric grid, a retained full-atlas internal buffer, and an explicit GPU idle/bubble can each appear nearly invariant when the final view rectangle changes. Some work is **scene-wide shared**, some **per family**, some **per view**, and some **per pixel**. They must not be conflated or the wrong optimization will be chosen.

**[SOURCE-READ — recommendation]** First test **one family containing two full-geometry eye foveae** plus **one separate one-view shared mono-context family** (two UE renderer submissions, three genuine views), rather than three singletons. The architecture is compatible with the documented `LUMEN_MAX_VIEWS=2` threshold; **three `FULL` views in one family are not a Lumen-correct shortcut**. The single-family 3-view alternative is useful only as a controlled diagnostic with Lumen disabled or patched by the game's actual engine. The 2+1 plan is still **NOT VERIFIED** with shipped STALKER 2's DLSS and camera/temporal ownership.

## 1. What really scales with resolution, view count, and scene?

**[SOURCE-READ]** Epic's [2026 multiview engine support breakdown](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/what-rendering-features-are-supported-in-splitscreen-with-multiple-views/2722648/2) is the strongest architectural source: independent Nanite cull/raster per split-screen view, shared VSM page pool but directional clipmaps per view, per-view HZB and fog volumes, per-view TSR history, per-view distance fields, *shared* ray-tracing TLAS, and first-view assumptions in selected sky/cloud/RT paths. The [VSM documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/virtual-shadow-maps-in-unreal-engine) explicitly distinguishes **Shadow Depths** (pages plus geometry/invalidations) from **Shadow Projection** (screen samples). The [Lumen performance guide](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/lumen-performance-guide-for-unreal-engine) documents amortized Surface Cache lighting updates. Do not add arbitrary GPU milliseconds to these components without a trace.

| **[SOURCE-READ]** VSM directional shadow depths / clipmaps | **Per visible view/light demands**, shared virtual physical-page pool, cached/invalidated pages. Pages are geometry/light dependent and are not the final 200×200 target. | Large/invalidated VSM pages may still be rasterized after reducing view color resolution. Lower output resolution can also lower page demand, so it is **not strictly invariant**. | **Conditionally:** `DynamicShadows` / direct-light or light-category flags may prevent some shadow setup **when installed before render-family setup**. There is **no ordinary `EngineShowFlags` bit to reduce only one family's VSM page resolution/clipmap LOD**; cannot switch `r.Shadow.Virtual.Enable` globally without affecting other work. |
| **[SOURCE-READ]** Conventional cascaded shadow depth (CSM) | Per-view cascades and scene-visible casters, shadow texture resolution set by light/shadow policy. | CSM shadow depth need not shrink with a tiny final color rect. | **Conditionally:** disable dynamic shadows or affecting light categories. Must verify active shadow method; STALKER 2 likely uses VSM in many scenes but this test's method is **NOT VERIFIED**. |
| **[SOURCE-READ]** Nanite visibility / instance + cluster culling | **Per split-screen view** culling/raster passes, scene complexity and number of visible clusters; GPU depth and coverage still matter. Not a no-risk optimization. |
| **[SOURCE-READ]** Ray-tracing scene / TLAS updates | Generally scene/frame shared acceleration-structure building, while effects dispatch per view. | **[SOURCE-READ]** Volumetric fog | **Per view**, with its own volume setup, injection, voxelization and integration; grid resolution and quality may not match 2D target size. | A minimum volumetric grid and light injection can remain costly. | **Yes, conditionally:** `VolumetricFog=false` per family; causes conspicuous fog mismatch (particularly in STALKER 2). A/B for diagnosis, not default production. |
| **[SOURCE-READ]** SkyAtmosphere LUTs, volumetric clouds, sky AO/cloud shadow | Mixture of scene/global LUTs, per-view cloud raymarch and **first-view-only cloud shadow/sky AO** according to Epic 2026. Not one fixed all-families cost. | LUT caching and cloud-shadow buffers need not resize with output. | **Partial:** `SkyAtmosphere`, `Atmosphere`, `VolumetricCloud`, `Fog` may skip respective *view* passes, **not guaranteed to suppress shared LUT/cache work** used by other families. |
| **[SOURCE-READ]** HZB and occlusion | **Per view** depth pyramid and occlusion/instance work, some dependent on scene visibility. | Hierarchical/culling overhead and fixed dispatches; output reduction may still reduce pyramid work. | **Limited:** `DisableOcclusionQueries` can turn off hardware occlusion queries but may **increase** visible geometry and is not a switch for all HZB/Nanite work. |

| **[SOURCE-READ]** DLSS / TSR / motion vectors and post-upscale | Per-view histories, output-size-sensitive work, **game/upscaler-specific feature state**, possible fixed dispatches. | DLSS may process physical output larger than reported 200×200 or retain a precreated feature. | **No per-family DLSS quality preset from show flags.** `AntiAliasing` / `TemporalAA` show flags are not safe substitutes for per-family NGX feature management. |
| **[SOURCE-READ]** RT barriers, resource clears, RDG graph passes, GPU waits | Some per-submission fixed overhead and sync; RDG setup itself is primarily **CPU**. | Repeating four renderer calls can create dispatch/barrier/synchronization floors. | **No blanket showflag**. Need GPU timeline to separate useful GPU work from idle wait and pipeline bubbles. |

**[SOURCE-READ]** An output rect of 200×200 is not proof that the renderer allocates or shades only 40,000 pixels. Build 51 creates four full-atlas `UTextureRenderTarget2D` sources (per previous [Build 51 source/target map](./2026-10-31-build51-map-for-3view.md)); Unreal's `FSceneTexturesConfig::Extent` is distinct from a view rect ([Epic API](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/Engine/FSceneTexturesConfig)). **NOT VERIFIED** if the internal GBuffer/Lumen/DLSS extents track the small rect, the full native source target, or another rounded/persistent extent in this STALKER build. This must be logged before interpreting the resolution sweep as scene complexity alone.

### Per-family options **without touching global CVars**

**[SOURCE-READ]** UE stores `FEngineShowFlags` on `FSceneViewFamily` (see [family API](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/Engine/FSceneViewFamily) and [ShowFlags API](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/Engine/FEngineShowFlags)). They are family-wide; they are **not** arbitrary per-rect or per-view overrides. Epic warns that **some show flags are fixed/compiled out in Shipping** and that show flags are not general-purpose scalability switches. A family-specific toggle is not equivalent to changing `r.Lumen.*`, `r.Shadow.Virtual.*`, `r.Nanite.*` for the entire game.

**[SOURCE-READ]** Highest-confidence, low-burden *diagnostic toggles*, in this order: (1) `MotionBlur`, `DepthOfField`, `Bloom`, `LensFlares` (test whether post dominates, usually not 7 ms at 200²); (2) `Decals`, `ScreenSpaceReflections`, `ScreenSpaceAO` / `AmbientOcclusion`; (3) `VolumetricFog` or `VolumetricCloud` (can change a lot in STALKER 2); (4) `DynamicShadows` (isolates shadow setup but breaks lighting); (5) `LumenReflections`, then `LumenGlobalIllumination` (isolates Lumen but causes illumination disparity); (6) disable most lighting **only as a performance floor diagnostic**. Never call a diagnostic visual degradation a usable production solution.

**[SOURCE-READ]** **Where to set these flags**: in a future patch, on a **distinct context family** at a validated **game-thread view-family construction/configuration hook** before UE snapshots the family into its renderer. For the Build 51 currently reused family/singleton path, [`QuadViewFamilyRouting.hpp#L204-L290`](https://github.com/liviutrn/UEVR-Quad-Views-Mono/blob/f4e086cde6d4a183cf2c1223dd9effaf6f8f7a77/quadview/QuadViewFamilyRouting.hpp#L204-L290) and [`apply_quadview_native_delivery.py#L241-L275`](https://github.com/liviutrn/UEVR-Quad-Views-Mono/blob/f4e086cde6d4a183cf2c1223dd9effaf6f8f7a77/quadview/apply_quadview_native_delivery.py#L241-L275) only replace `Views` and `RenderTarget`; they **do not** give an already-created singleton an independent show-flag set. Temporarily changing the *same family* around an RT callback may be **too late** (some decisions baked at GT setup), and unsafe with asynchronous processing and copied `FViewInfo`. One needs authoritative UE5.5 SDK members and a scoped save/restore path with no other live family sharing that state. **NOT VERIFIED** that the restricted SDK used in this title exposes a safe `FEngineShowFlags` writer.

**[SOURCE-READ — performance warning]** Disabling periphery Lumen, fog or shadows changes the radiance and geometry cues at the fovea boundary. The desired periphery is *one shared low-resolution picture behind two real-eye foveae*, so lighting consistency and shared exposure may matter more than raw GPU ms. Use the toggles diagnostically first; either keep visually significant features or document the visible seam penalty.

## 3. `FULL` independent views versus `SECONDARY` stereo

**[SOURCE-READ]** `EStereoscopicPass::eSSP_FULL` denotes a non-stereo pass; `PRIMARY` needs its own pass; `SECONDARY` can be instanced with a primary ([Epic enum](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/Engine/EStereoscopicPass)). UE supports conventional multiple `FULL` split-screen views, each with independent `FSceneViewStateInterface`, projection, rect and camera, all in a single `FSceneViewFamily`. Independent histories are conceptually possible, but **family show flags and the render target remain shared**.

**[SOURCE-READ — evidence for a real secondary difference]** In public UE **5.4-era** desktop renderer source excerpt [`Renderer/Private/PostProcess/PostProcessing.cpp` around line 601](https://indxzero.github.io/ue544cvarwiki/articles/r.fastvram.eyeadaptation/), the eye-adaptation pass has:
`bEyeAdaptationEnabled = bHasViewState && bPrimaryView;` with the explicit comment **"Skip for secondary views in a stereo setup"**. The same excerpt says disabling the eye-adaptation show flag still runs a clamped pass. This **directly supports why a stereo-secondary may not maintain its own exposure adaptation**. **NOT VERIFIED** that STALKER 2's modified 5.5 desktop renderer uses that exact line or that its bright output came from that line. **`FULL` can give independent eye adaptation if it is treated as a primary independent view and owns a persistent view state**, unlike a stereo-secondary. Independently metered eye foveae + mono periphery can create a visible exposure seam: independent exposure is not by itself the desired final visual result.

**[SOURCE-READ]** UE's [`FSceneView` API](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/Engine/FSceneView) exposes `GetPrimarySceneView`, `GetSecondaryViews`, `GetInstancedSceneView`, `IsPrimarySceneView`, `IsInstancedStereoPass`, `HasEyeAdaptationViewState`, `GetLastEyeAdaptationExposure`, `PreExposure` and separate `State`. These are **concrete code-path predicates and measurements** to add to a runtime trace before making a universal claim about which passes a secondary skips. In an older renderer excerpt, tonemapping LUT generation is primary-only and secondary can reuse a primary LUT; **current UE5.5 LUT behavior is NOT VERIFIED**. The correct statement is **some primary/secondary postprocess state differs**, not "all secondary lighting or shadows are omitted."

**[SOURCE-READ]** **Lumen**: Epic 2026 confirms `LUMEN_MAX_VIEWS=2` and `ShouldRenderLumenForViewFamily()` rejecting `Views.Num()>2` (except cube capture); even with two split-screen views, certain paths assume `ViewIndex=0` and mesh-SDF culling can fail for distant cameras ([Epic post](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/what-rendering-features-are-supported-in-splitscreen-with-multiple-views/2722648/2)). **`FULL` does not override the >2-family view-count limit.** A second stereo-secondary is **not universally excluded from Lumen**; count and camera/scene policy matter. In the separate singleton render calls of Build 51, the renderer sees one view per callback.

**[SOURCE-READ]** **Shadows**: Epic describes VSM directional clipmaps per view, local-light shadow maps selected from the closest view, CSM cascades per view, and shared VSM physical pool. A stereo-secondary can reuse data through primary/instanced-stereo heuristics; `FULL` split-screen views are more independent, which may **increase** view-dependent work (especially Nanite culling and VSM clipmap demand) rather than reduce it. **No source-read UE5.5 rule proves secondary = no shadows**. Test shadow depth and projection independently; brightness discrepancy does not identify its cause.

**[SOURCE-READ]** **DLSS / TSR**: Epic 2026 reports `FTSRHistory` independent per split-screen view, but the shipped DLSS integration is not independently inspected; `FULL` does **not** guarantee separate NGX features/history, camera jitter or resource ownership. Verify per-role DLSS evaluations, history reset and exposure inputs on the actual 2+1 proposal.

**[SOURCE-READ]** **Project-specific root of Build 40 parity risk**: [Build 40 `apply_quadviews.py#L190-L211`](https://github.com/liviutrn/UEVR-Quad-Views-Mono/blob/7486fc59890b14673cd50770b0dafeb692576716/quadview/apply_quadviews.py#L190-L211) intentionally disables Native Stereo Fix in ID7; Mono's [`apply_stage234.py#L643-L665`](https://github.com/liviutrn/UEVR-Quad-Views-Mono/blob/f4e086cde6d4a183cf2c1223dd9effaf6f8f7a77/duoview/joey_stage1/apply_stage234.py#L643-L665) relabels the eligible **singleton** second constructor from `SECONDARY` to `PRIMARY` for independent shading. Transplanting that relabeling into a simultaneous 3+ view family **does not cure Lumen's >2 limit** and is not proof that all four views can receive unique exposure and GI histories.

## 4. Build 51's actual "family" accounting and the low-risk experiment

**[SOURCE-READ]** Build 51's [`dispatch_main_views`](https://github.com/liviutrn/UEVR-Quad-Views-Mono/blob/f4e086cde6d4a183cf2c1223dd9effaf6f8f7a77/quadview/QuadViewFamilyRouting.hpp#L204-L290) takes a frozen **four-view** `FSceneViewFamily` and performs **four render-module callbacks** after temporarily changing its view count to **one** and switching its `RenderTarget`. [`apply_quadview_native_delivery.py#L241-L275`](https://github.com/liviutrn/UEVR-Quad-Views-Mono/blob/f4e086cde6d4a183cf2c1223dd9effaf6f8f7a77/quadview/apply_quadview_native_delivery.py#L241-L275) calls the original renderer for the first batch and a singleton renderer callback for each remaining slot; it even decrements the scene frame counter for later slots. This is **four actual renderer invocations**, despite one original outer GT family identity. Thus referring to all four as "a single family" when discussing GPU work is misleading. **NOT VERIFIED** whether the repeated invocations duplicate every scene-wide GPU operation, reuse cache, or stall at specific RDG transitions; measure rather than assume.

**[SOURCE-READ — comparison arithmetic, not a prediction]** If the measurements represent comparable GPU work and simple additive critical-path costs, then three separate one-view callbacks might cost `3×7 ≈21 ms`, while **a 2-view fovea callback + one mono-context callback** would correspond to `(7+2.5)+7 ≈16.5 ms`, a nominal **~4.5 ms difference**. This is **NOT a forecast or verified cost**: the experiments use a special 200×200 configuration, distinct frusta and shader paths may change costs, GPU tasks can overlap, and the full game resolution differs. Only use it to motivate a measurement.

**[SOURCE-READ — specific one-change experiment queue]**

1. **Instrument before optimizing:** At the same deterministic stationary scene, for each render callback record view count, family/scene/state pointers, pass label, `ViewRect`, `FSceneTexturesConfig::Extent` when obtainable, physical DXGI color/depth descriptors, Lumen enablement, DLSS evaluation dimensions/history ID, VSM page counts, and GPU total plus graphics/compute timeline. Confirm whether the reported 200×200 corresponds to internal shading rather than just the composite rectangle.
2. **Validate command access:** Try `stat unit`, `stat gpu` via UEVR homebrew console; try `ProfileGPU` through the native console or a timed `user_script.txt`. If single-token homebrew input is required, fix only the one-token dispatcher (separate explicit development approval). If Shipping lacks profilers, use an external D3D12 capture.
3. **Find dominant per-callback work:** In one fixed context-family test, toggle one source of GPU work per run: first VSM/dynamic shadows, then Lumen, then fog/clouds, then Nanite/fallback **diagnostically**, without also changing resolution or pass type. Record feature's removed *actual GPU pass* rather than just total frame FPS. Restore baseline after each experiment.
4. **Prove 2+1:** Compare 3 singletons against a **two-view `FULL`/split-screen-style fovea family + one full-FOV mono context singleton**, preserving three independent, persistent states and source-frame alignment. Compare native per-eye shading and whether DLSS executes independently. Use correct view rectangles in a shared target, avoid stale history/camera ownership, and keep Lumen family view count ≤2. If full split-screen foveae lose important stereo optimizations, A/B a valid `PRIMARY+SECONDARY` foveal pair instead.
5. **Only then** consider cheaper context-family flags (fog, SSR, AO, reflections) individually, accepting a change only if the **composited headset result has no unacceptable lighting seam** and P95 GPU time improves. Do **not** interleave global CVars between in-flight render callbacks.

**[SOURCE-READ — acceptance criteria]** Deliver profiler pass names/timestamps or equivalent GPU capture evidence, actual per-callback geometry sizes, exact view-state separation, Lumen presence, matching output exposure, per-eye DLSS color/depth/motion inputs, and at least 100 stable gameplay frames. Report P50/P95 GPU ms plus source provenance. Distinguish render-thread elapsed time, GPU queue busy duration, and wall-clock display frame time. **No new CI build, external capture, GPU timing, or HMD test was executed in this research.**

## 5. Critical review and go/no-go

**[SOURCE-READ]** The most dangerous assumptions are **(a)** "7 ms is inherently unavoidable" (might be shadow-page work or a full internal buffer), **(b)** "show flags can turn off all VSM/Lumen scene work just for context" (they cannot guarantee that), **(c)** "`stat gpu` in a Shipping game must show timings if UEVR has a console" (false), **(d)** "three `FULL` views preserve Lumen in one family" (documented >2 cutoff), and **(e)** "independent exposure per eye automatically improves a foveated composite" (it may produce seams).

**[SOURCE-READ]** **Decision:** proceed to implementation **only after a profiler-access/gpu-size proof** or an explicit acceptance of the remaining unknowns; preferentially prototype **2+1 families** with realistic per-eye foveal shading. Keep periphery lighting faithful until concrete per-pass numbers justify lowering a feature. This avoids simultaneously changing view layout, scene histories, global shader quality, and output composition, which would make the next headset report impossible to diagnose.
